## Proposal for a 5% Global Minimum Commission for Validators

** Rationale Behind a 5% Minimum Commission**

**1**.** Aligning with TFL Community Grants:** The TFL community grant program only recognizes validators with a 5%–10% commission. This criterion inherently discourages a 0% commission model. In this landscape, a 0% commission becomes impractical and obsolete. The TFL community grant program is a fair and smart way to encourage validators to participate in healthy competition and helps everyone involved do better.

**2. Empowering Smaller Validators:** The intention is to level the playing field for all validators within the ecosystem, especially the smaller ones. Introducing a minimum 5% rate will give these smaller validators a fair shot at attracting delegators. This approach ensures these smaller validators can generate income for their operations, fostering a more inclusive and balanced environment for growth.

**3. Changing how the delegators make decisions:** Under the current system, delegators often pick validators based on how much money they make. This is what we change by setting a minimum fee. We ask delegators to think about more important things, like how good the validators' infrastructure is, how involved they are in the community and government, and what they bring to the network. This proposal aims to create an ecosystem where choices are made based on worth and contribution, not just cost.

**4. The Importance of Robust Validators:** Validators must be robust to ensure reliable services. This robustness comes not just from strong hardware and infrastructure but also from sustainable revenue streams. A financially stable validator is better equipped to invest in necessary upgrades and maintenance, ensuring ongoing service quality and network integrity.

**5. Creating a healthier competitive environment:** By making it impossible to get a zero commission, we stop a bad race to the bottom in commission rates. Instead, we support a more fair and healthy level of competition where validators are rewarded for doing a better job and adding more to the network.

## **Understanding the Pitfalls of 0% Commission:**

**1. Financial Unsustainability:** Validators operating without a commission face financial challenges. This approach is not just impractical; it's a path leading to potential service instability.

**2. Risk to Users:** The allure of zero costs often attracts new users to these validators. However, this can be misleading. 0% commission validators might initially draw in delegators, only to abruptly hike their fees later. This practice isn't just deceptive; it undermines user trust in the system.

In conclusion, by introducing a minimum commission, we are not just creating robust validators, we are safeguarding the interests of users, ensuring a fair system for all participants in the network. The sustainability of validators is fundamental to the overall health and security of the network. Ensuring that validators have the means to cover their operational costs and invest in continuous improvements is essential for a robust and reliable blockchain ecosystem.
