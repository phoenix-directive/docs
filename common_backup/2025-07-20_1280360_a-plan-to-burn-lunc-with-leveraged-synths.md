Hello everyone, I am a strong believer LUNC can become exciting again but something extraordinary has to be done. The biggest goal I think should be to burn the supply which will lead to inflationary pressure. The current burn rate is negligable.
Project Forge is a built on a simple premise: You permanently burn LUNC, and in return, you get immediate, amplified exposure to LUNC.
The process is direct. A user sends their LUNC to the "Forge" smart contract, which does two things in a single, irreversible transaction:
100% Burn: The contract immediately sends the LUNC to the official burn address, permanently removing it from the supply.
Minting a Synth: The contract mints a new, synthetic token back to the user's wallet. Users can choose their level of conviction:
LUNCx2 (2x Leverage)
LUNCx5 (5x Leverage)
LUNCx10 (10x Leverage)
This mechanism is a one-way street. You cannot redeem LUNCx back for LUNC through the protocol. The burn is final. This brings us to the most important question>
**How Leverage is Maintained**
The 2x, 5x, and 10x leverage will be maintained through an elastic supply (rebase) algorithm. At regular intervals (e.g., 3 seconds), the protocol checks the price of LUNC. It then automatically adjusts the number of LUNCx tokens in every wallet to match the leveraged performance. If LUNC goes up 5%, your LUNCx10 balance increases by 50%. If LUNC goes down 5%, your balance decreases by 50%.
How You Realize Value
The LUNCx tokens are your reward, and their value is realized on the open market. To take profits or exit a position, you would: > Go to a DEX > Find the Liquidity Pool: Look for a trading pair for your asset, for example, LUNCx10 / wUSDC or LUNCx10 / LUNC.
Swap Your Token: Execute a normal swap to trade your LUNCx for a stablecoin, LUNC, or any other asset available in the pool.
This means the success of Project Forge is directly tied to our community's ability to create and incentivize these crucial liquidity pools, which is a key topic for our discussion.

Also a predefined backstop can be added to the mechanism for example > backstop at 100 million LUNC.

What do you think? What other technical challenges or risks should we be considering?
