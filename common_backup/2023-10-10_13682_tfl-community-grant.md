**Background**

Before UST’s depeg in May 2022, Terra had one of the most vibrant ecosystems in all of Web3. Following the depeg, TFL and the Terra community came together and agreed that the ecosystem we had built was worth preserving. When the community relaunched Terra, it was done in hopes of capturing all of the energy and momentum of the previous ecosystem/community into a new chain, absent the stablecoin. A large portion of the tokens were put into the Community Pool to fund its growth.

Since then, the bear market ensued, and projects and liquidity have disappeared. At this time, LUNA can no longer sustainably support the funding of new and existing projects/initiatives. The builders who are left are stuck in a tragedy of the commons where, instead of collaborating to improve the ecosystem, even non-competitive projects are forced to compete for limited Community Pool funding. 

This has two major impacts:

- LUNA has more sell pressure than necessary, making it hard for the community to gain momentum
- The community/ecosystem is in a constant state of conflict


We need to end that conflict, pull together as a community, and jumpstart the Terra ecosystem without adding additional sell pressure to LUNA.

**Where we can all agree**

- Terra builders need some support through the bear market. They need to find their own path to product-market-fit (PMF), but we should acknowledge that due to UST’s fallout, they face unique challenges they wouldn’t encounter in other ecosystems
- Those builders who stick around and build useful products (as measured by user retention and engagement) should participate in Terra’s success
- We need to invest aggressively to build useful/fun products for our community and outcompete our competition
- If we try to fund all of this through the Community Pool, we will not be successful


**How TFL can help**

TFL can solve this problem, but we need the community's support. (Reminder: TFL never received an allocation of LUNA from Terra’s relaunch) To date, we’ve been building using the treasury left over after the depeg. Our non-LUNA treasury can be deployed to support Terra ecosystem growth, but it’s limited, and we need to ensure that if we spend those funds in service of LUNA, we have a treasury that 1) benefits from our efforts, and 2) supports TFL for years/decades into the future. We published a [proposal](https://agora.terra.money/discussion/12104-terra-expansion-grant-revised-final) before with some of our thoughts, but we weren't clear enough about how we could help solve this problem. This is an update to that proposal to more clearly outline why we think this makes sense for Terra.

**Blueprint: How we can jumpstart the Terra ecosystem without spending LUNA**

- TFL to negotiate and pay for community tools/projects using its non-LUNA treasury, ensuring accountability within an enforceable legal framework. We will conduct due diligence on the project’s potential value-add to the ecosystem and its ability to execute. This will relieve a significant burden on the community.


- TFL to provide _Foundation_ (best-in-class, high-performance infra/indexers/relayers) to all credible builders on Terra. Existing Cosmos infrastructure is expensive and lacks scalability. Indexers are a hassle. We've taken everything we learned running Terra at scale and turned it into a free product for our ecosystem. This significantly reduces protocol operating expenses and gives our ecosystem some help and support while they find PMF.
- Rate limiting and reasonableness apply; TFL reserves the right to deny service


- TFL to provide LUNA incentives to teams that drive fees and user engagement within Terra. These incentives will vest based on time and total LUNA market cap, avoiding short to medium-term price issues while giving founders skin in the game and upside exposure when we all succeed. 


- TFL to provide high-quality collateral to the Terra ecosystem and partners to solve the cold start problem. We can bring in tokens like BTC, ETH, stablecoins, and others, matching LUNA from the Community Pool to provide liquidity on Terra or other chains, or simply deploy the collateral on its own. With the help of the Terra community, this collateral will be allocated to opportunities that maximize transaction volume, capital velocity, user retention, and economic activity.


- Implement Fee Share Module (separate proposal): TFL to match 50% of fees generated by Terra applications with stablecoins until Jan. 2025, enabling ecosystem teams to earn 75% of the fees they generate on-chain. There is nothing more value aligning than this. We need to focus on usage, engagement, and retention – capital, network effects, and brand equity will follow. 


- Lock the remainder of the Terra Community Pool until Jan. 2025 (separate proposal) and then revisit it as a community. TFL can develop this over one week and post a subsequent governance proposal to upgrade the chain, implementing the change.


- Lastly, TFL will continue to operate as more than just an L1 foundation (akin to a combination of the Ethereum Foundation and Consensys), supporting the chain, offering both financial and non-financial support to ecosystem teams, organizing ecosystem events, driving awareness of the ecosystem and its projects, and building out products like Enterprise, Warp, Alliance, Station, JAX, Tashkent, Verity, and Quill – all in service of LUNA stakeholders.

Proposed Shift from Community to TFL Based:

The proposition of transitioning Terra2.0 from a Community-owned Network to a TFL-based one deviates from the foundational ethos of Terra's creation. This move mirrors the structure of version 1, signifying a reversal from Terra2.0's launch principles. It is acknowledged that without TFL's active involvement, Terra's network vitality is at risk. However, the request for a near 15% supply allocation to TFL seems to undermine the initial intent of maintaining a community-centric network, even as TFL continues to contribute.

Community Pool Lock Concerns:

The past 18 months have witnessed suboptimal advancements, with limited high-quality products emerging from TFL or other contributors. Despite these challenges, the proposal to lock the community pool appears premature. An alternative could be to request a specific amount of Luna upon achieving particular project milestones. While the idea of a legally binding contract for grants is favorable, the major chunk of spending from "undelivered teams" originated from the Emergency fund led by a TFL proposal, making the community pool lock seem unwarranted. There's evidence to suggest that genuine community expenditures, like TFM and Lion DAO, have fostered user retention and contributed positively to Terra.

Selective Staking and Governance Power:

The proposal mentions Luna staking without voting power, hinting at potential conflicts of interest, especially for validators listed due to "TFL delegation." Historically, TFL has been proactive in pushing its proposals, raising concerns about safeguarding community interests and network decentralization. With nearly 500 million total tokens staked, a 25 million TFL stake could significantly influence the network dynamics, potentially swaying validators towards supporting TFL-proposed initiatives.

Transparency Demands:

Despite the assertion of ongoing dialogue, the lack of complete transparency on grant spendings is concerning. If certain agreements are made confidentially due to third-party preferences, it raises questions on TFL's commitment to maintaining transparency with the community.

Excessive Grant Request:

The rationale behind the 150 million Luna figure remains unclear, especially as it represents a 50 million increase from the last proposal without apparent justification or detailed disclosure from TFL. It's imperative to understand how this figure was derived and what benchmarks justify this substantial grant request.

Recommendations:

1. Hold off on the current proposal, presuming TFL has sufficient resources to manage its projects.
2a. Consider introducing a bonus mechanism to reward team members upon achieving major milestones for flagship projects via specific proposals.
2b. Propose grants for specific phases of flagship products, linked to tangible deliverables.
2c. Re-evaluate this proposal in another 18 months or earlier if TFL's resources deplete. 

These suggestions aim to maintain a balance between TFL’s contributions and the original community-centric vision, ensuring Terra’s sustainable and collaborative growth.
